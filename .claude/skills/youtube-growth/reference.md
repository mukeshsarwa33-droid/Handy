# Reference: growth rules used by the youtube-growth skill

These are working rules, written in this skill's own words. Check them against
the channel's own data before relying on them.

## Outliers

An outlier is a video that gets roughly 3x to 30x its channel's average views.
Outliers show what the audience chooses to click. When studying one, record:

- the format (how the video is structured, for example a question put to strangers,
  a challenge with a deadline, a reveal)
- the title shape (not the wording: the pattern, such as a bold claim followed by a
  twist)
- the thumbnail composition (camera angle, what is close to the lens, the emotion,
  how many text words)
- the stakes (what is at risk, and how it is shown)

Good ideas combine parts from outliers in different niches. A format from one
niche, a title pattern from another, and a visual from a third can make a new
idea that no single creator has made. Do not reuse specific wording or footage.

## Floor and ceiling

- **Floor:** the views the core audience gives an ordinary video. Use the median
  of the last 10 to 20 uploads, not the mean, because one big hit distorts the mean.
- **Ceiling:** the total audience that could plausibly care about one specific idea.
  Estimate it from the niche, not from the channel.

An idea should have a ceiling that clearly exceeds the floor. Ideas with a ceiling
near the floor are safe, but they cannot grow the channel.

## Topic overlap (the 80% rule)

Most of a channel's content should be on the core topic. Roughly 80% of the time,
the next video should be a close neighbor of the last few. Adjacent topics often
overlap less than expected, because viewers of one niche rarely follow the other.
When proposing a bridge topic, state the estimated overlap and the reason it might
work.

## Audience tiers (CCN)

- **Core:** for superfans. Works for personal updates, series, and insider content.
  Usually has a low ceiling.
- **Casual:** for regular viewers. A familiar format that is easy to follow.
- **New:** for people who do not follow the channel. A universal challenge, clear
  stakes, and a result anyone can understand in a few seconds. Usually has the highest
  ceiling and is the main source of growth.

Use the mix to decide the next batch. If a channel's batch is all Core, growth will
stall. If it is all New, the channel may lose its regulars.

## Titles

Search is a minor share of traffic for most channels, so titles are written to win
the homepage click first, and then to be findable.

Three levels for each idea:

1. **Plain:** states the facts. Accurate but flat.
2. **Story plus superlative:** adds a personal angle and a "best" or "cheapest" style claim.
3. **Contradiction:** adds a tension that the video resolves, such as a claim followed
   by a reason it might not hold.

Recommend level 2 or 3 when the video delivers on it. Level 3 must be paid off in the
video, or it becomes clickbait.

Formatting: title case for major words. Avoid all-caps, which reduces readability at
a glance.

## Thumbnails

Describe, do not design in detail:

- what is visible and how close it is
- the emotion on the face, if a face is used
- at most three to four words of text, or none
- the one thing a viewer should understand in half a second

## Stakes

Stakes must match the channel's tone.

- **Action stakes:** a visible goal with a cost of failure. Works for entertainment.
  Scale it: a small goal adds mild interest, a doubter adds more, a large prize adds the most.
- **Information stakes:** the cost of not knowing. Works for educational and finance-style
  channels, for example the difference a choice makes over time, stated plainly.

Do not use physical penalties or stunts on a channel whose tone is calm or educational.

## Priorities over time (the pendulum)

1. **Watchability first:** clear audio, sharp video, no echo.
2. **Packaging and ideas:** titles, thumbnails, and concepts. Most growth happens here.
3. **Production:** better sets, cameras, and editing. Worth investing in only after
   packaging works. Expensive videos that nobody clicks do not help.

Recommend production work only if the analytics show good click-through but poor
retention, or the profile says packaging is already strong.

## Reading the analytics

- **Click-through rate (CTR):** low CTR with good retention means the packaging
  (title or thumbnail) is the problem.
- **Average view duration:** low retention with good CTR means the promise is not
  delivered, or the opening is slow.
- **Views from browse and suggested:** these show homepage packaging is working.
- **Search share:** if it is high, the topic has demand but limited new viewers.

Compare each metric to the channel's own median, not to general benchmarks.
